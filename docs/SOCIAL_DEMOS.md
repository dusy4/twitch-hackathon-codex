# Viral demo concepts and proof artifacts

Don't over-optimize for generic AI buzzwords. The viral pattern is a *familiar medium doing something apparently impossible*, documented with real footage. Produce real clips after product implementation and streamer's consent; no fake UI/camera success as a production demonstration.

1. **The portal invasion (12–20s):** Start on normal live Twitch screen. A live raid notification triggers actual black-violet portal, 200+ on-screen raiders as instanced visual crowd, known users appear individually with names. One instantly waves and leaves a trail. Opening text: "Twitch raids don't have to be a notification."
2. **The dog trick (12–20s):** Real camera dog walking. Chat input `!rift sprint towards the dog and sit on it` visible. Tiny avatar spots dog, sprints and mounts dog silhouette with joyful bounce, dynamically follows. Onscreen text: "I gave Twitch chat bodies inside real life." No real dog is directed or affected.
3. **The red hat (12–18s):** Avatar runs after a red-hatted *visual object*, continues moving with screen track and loses target on camera cut; show precise grounded behavior vs hardcoded movement.
4. **A crowd physically builds a castle (15–25s):** Each player's individual 3D avatar places blocks while streamer is IRL walking; time-lapse world building, portal particles and applause.
5. **Cross-stream world transfer (20–30s):** Split screen source/destination creators; initiating raid causes synchronized portals, unique avatars and faction banner migrate, persistent usernames/gear unaffected.
6. **Quest + world leaderboard (15–25s):** Camera sees bicycle; streamer completes approved quest, award transaction confirmed, viewer leaderboard jumps and changes avatar cosmetic instantly.
7. **Moving-camera AR wow (15–25s):** Streamer pans phone from floor to wall; portal remains attached and avatar goes behind foreground bench with occlusion; label as native AR version.

## Capture and sharing requirements
OBS/local capture with readable chat and mouse/keyboard display where useful; no more than one narrative fact per shot, visible original action, 15–25s primary variant with no audio required, sound-rich variant for Twitch; text overlays legible on mobile, include product attribution in credits, obtain streamer consent before saving/distributing footage. Produce 9:16 and 16:9 edits, actual public playable link and product site leaderboard link. Track demo views, visits, Twitch connects and actions per participant rather than views alone.
